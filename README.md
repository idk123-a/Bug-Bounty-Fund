# Proposal: Universal Smart Contract Security & Bug Bounty Fund

## Proposal

Establish the concept of a universal security and bug bounty funding mechanism for the Qubic Smart Contract ecosystem.

The purpose of this proposal is to determine whether there is sufficient **general Computor support to develop and implement this concept further**.

This is **not a final proposal for the exact percentages, funding rules or implementation**. If supported, a second proposal would define and vote on the final parameters.

## Available Options

> **Option 0:** Do not support the concept

> **Option 1:** Support the concept and further development

---

## Background

The Qubic Smart Contract ecosystem is growing and Smart Contracts are increasingly becoming part of critical infrastructure.

As more value, bridges and external assets enter the ecosystem, the potential impact of a vulnerability also increases.

At the moment, security work such as audits and bug bounties can depend heavily on voluntary donations from developers, shareholders or community members.

This is difficult to sustain.

Individual Smart Contracts may not yet generate enough revenue to continuously finance security work, especially during periods of low adoption or unfavorable market conditions.

The goal is therefore to move towards a **proactive and sustainable security funding mechanism**, rather than repeatedly raising donations when a vulnerability is discovered or when an audit becomes necessary.

---

## Initial Concept

The initial idea is that a small portion of future Smart Contract IPO proceeds could be allocated to ecosystem security instead of being burned.

The currently discussed example is:

| Allocation | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **95%** | Existing IPO burn |
| **3%** | Universal Smart Contract Bug Bounty Fund |
| **2%** | CCF |

The **3% Bug Bounty Fund** would be a shared ecosystem fund that could finance:

- Bug bounties
- Security audits and reviews
- Independent security research
- Testnet security competitions
- Other proactive Smart Contract security activities

The intention is that security should be funded **before** a major incident occurs, rather than relying on emergency donations afterwards.

---

## Fund Cap

To prevent the fund from accumulating an unnecessarily large balance, an initial maximum balance of **100 billion QUBIC** could be established.

Once the fund reaches this limit, the corresponding allocation could instead be redirected to CCF until the fund falls below the cap.

The exact mechanism would be defined in the follow-up proposal.

---

## Eligibility

Smart Contracts participating in the program would need to meet basic security standards.

The initial idea includes requirements such as:

- Appropriate testing
- Basic technical documentation
- A clearly defined security contact
- Responsible vulnerability disclosure
- Independent security review or audit where appropriate

The detailed eligibility criteria and bounty rules would also be defined in the subsequent proposal.

---

## Existing Smart Contracts

The initial mechanism would naturally apply to future Smart Contract IPOs.

Existing Smart Contracts could also be included in the Universal Bug Bounty Program, subject to the eligibility requirements and a suitable onboarding mechanism.

For example, existing projects could initially join by making a **one-time membership contribution** to the Universal Bug Bounty Fund. The amount and exact conditions would be determined in the subsequent proposal.

This would allow the fund to support important existing infrastructure as well as new projects, while giving existing Smart Contracts a way to participate before the new IPO mechanism is in place.

---

## Why a Universal Fund?

Security vulnerabilities do not necessarily remain isolated to one project.

A vulnerability in infrastructure such as an exchange, bridge, vault or other high-value Smart Contract could have consequences for users and confidence across the wider ecosystem.

A shared security fund therefore allows the ecosystem to contribute collectively to protecting infrastructure that can ultimately benefit the entire Qubic ecosystem.

It also reduces the need for individual projects to repeatedly ask shareholders and community members for additional donations when security work is required.

---

## Further Development

This proposal intentionally does **not** attempt to finalize every detail.

A positive result would mean that the Computors support the **general direction** and believe that a universal Smart Contract security funding mechanism is worth developing further.

A subsequent proposal would then address and put to a separate vote:

- Final IPO allocation percentages
- Fund cap
- Membership contribution for existing Smart Contracts
- Governance
- Eligibility requirements
- Bounty criteria and payout limits
- Administration of the fund
- Treatment of existing Smart Contracts
- Sunset/review conditions
- Technical implementation

---

## Important

A **YES** vote on this proposal **does not approve the proposed 95% / 3% / 2% allocation, the 100 billion QUBIC cap, the membership contribution, or any of the other parameters described above.**

These are presented only as the **initial concept currently being discussed**.

The purpose of this vote is simply to determine:

> **Is there sufficient Computor support to develop this concept into a detailed proposal for a Universal Smart Contract Security & Bug Bounty Fund?**
